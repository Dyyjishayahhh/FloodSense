1. Document Overview
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the proposed functional, data, technical, safety, and validation requirements for FloodSense before software development begins. It replaces implementation claims in the earlier feature specification with a planning baseline that can be reviewed by the research team, technical adviser, thesis adviser, and prospective institutional data providers.
1.2 Proposed Product Scope
FloodSense will be a scenario-based Android application intended to help Bacoor City residents understand generalized flood susceptibility and prepare before a flood event. The proposed core will use a rule-based Expert System, a PostgreSQL/PostGIS database, a backend application programming interface (API), and a Flutter mobile client. The system will not function as an official forecast, live warning service, risk model, or independent evacuation authority.
1.3 Planning Status
1.4 Governing Assumptions
The project will prioritize a working, explainable Expert System before optional conversational AI.
Results will be described as scenario-based susceptibility estimates rather than real-time safety declarations.
Geographic precision will not exceed the precision of the approved spatial and historical data.
Institutional collaboration, data release, expert validation, and evacuation-center accuracy will be treated as pending until documented.
Provisional demonstration data may be used during development only when clearly labelled and excluded from final accuracy claims.

4.2 DOST-ASTI Rainfall Data Planning Note
DOST-ASTI has stated that most stations were installed around 2012 and that data may be provided from station installation until the last transmission. The provider stated that records before 2023 are generally transmitted at 15-minute intervals and records from 2023 onward at 10-minute intervals. Rainfall and water-level variables may be available depending on station type and location. Exact Bacoor or nearest-station coverage, file structure, units, missing periods, quality flags, and licensing remain to be confirmed.
The raw 10-minute and 15-minute observations will be preserved unchanged.
Processing will calculate comparable accumulations such as 1-hour, 3-hour, 6-hour, 12-hour, and 24-hour totals after units and accumulation behavior are verified.
Station observations will not be treated as representative of all Bacoor areas without geographic and expert justification.
Rainfall records will not independently determine susceptibility; they must be paired with flood-event evidence and expert review.
4.3 Data Quality and Provenance Requirements
Every dataset shall record its provider, request/reference number, date received, license, permitted use, geographic coverage, temporal coverage, units, and update date.
Original source files shall be preserved separately from cleaned or derived data.
Missing, estimated, corrected, or rejected values shall be explicitly flagged.
No provisional or unverified dataset shall be presented to users as official or current.
Every rule shall link to its evidence sources and expert review record.
5. Expert System Requirements
5.1 Selected Expert System Type
FloodSense will use a rule-based production system represented through decision tables and evaluated using forward chaining. The engine will begin with facts obtained from the user scenario, geographic lookup, and the approved zone profile. It will then identify matching rules, resolve applicable conflicts, and return an explainable conclusion. This approach is proposed because it is transparent, reviewable by subject-matter experts, and does not depend on a large labelled training dataset.
5.2 Expert System Components
5.3 Proposed Fact Model
Resolved geographic zone and parent barangay.
Rainfall intensity category and applicable official threshold version.
Rainfall duration or verified accumulation interval.
Static baseline susceptibility or approved zone profile attributes.
Historical flood-frequency/depth category where supported.
Rule-set version and evaluation timestamp.
5.4 Knowledge Acquisition and Rule Construction
Define the geographic units supported by available evidence.
Create historical cases linking rainfall observations to dated and located flood records.
Present the cleaned cases and maps to designated experts through a structured elicitation form or workshop.
Record proposed conditions, classifications, exceptions, uncertainty, and preparedness implications.
Convert the accepted combinations into a decision table.
Assign a rule identifier, source links, reviewer, approval status, priority, confidence, version, and effective date.
Test the proposed rules against historical or expert-labelled cases not used during construction.
Activate only rules that meet the approved validation and sign-off criteria.
5.5 Forward-Chaining Inference Procedure
5.6 Conflict Resolution and Uncertainty
A sub-zone rule shall take precedence over a barangay-wide fallback rule when both are approved and applicable.
A rule with all required conditions satisfied shall take precedence over a more general rule.
Only approved and active rules may produce public-facing classifications.
The current effective version shall take precedence over superseded versions.
An expert-assigned priority may be used only when its rationale is recorded.
If equally applicable rules disagree, the system shall return Uncertain and shall not silently choose the more severe or more convenient output.
If no rule applies, the system shall return Insufficient Data and display the limitation.

8. Proposed Technical Architecture
The proposed architecture separates the Android client from data storage and inference. The Flutter application will communicate with a secured backend API. The API will validate requests, resolve spatial locations, invoke the Expert System service, retrieve approved preparedness content, and access PostgreSQL/PostGIS. The mobile application will not connect directly to the database.

10. Verification and Validation Plan
10.1 Requirements Verification
Maintain a requirements traceability matrix linking each requirement to design elements and tests.
Conduct unit tests for rainfall normalization, spatial lookup, rule matching, conflict resolution, and explanations.
Conduct integration tests across Flutter, API, Expert System service, PostgreSQL/PostGIS, and offline cache.
Conduct failure tests for missing data, unsupported locations, stale caches, network loss, and server errors.
10.2 Expert System Validation
Have designated experts independently review the proposed facts, conditions, classifications, exceptions, and explanations.
Reserve historical or expert-labelled cases that were not used to construct the rules.
Run the system against the reserved cases and compare its classifications with the reference assessment.
Report rule coverage, agreement, confusion matrix, and accuracy/precision/recall/F1 where the number and quality of labelled cases permit.
Use an appropriate agreement statistic when multiple independent experts label the same cases.
Test exact thresholds, zone boundaries, conflicting rules, missing facts, and unsupported scenarios.
Activate only rules that satisfy the approved acceptance criteria and recorded sign-off process.
10.3 User and Safety Evaluation
Evaluate whether users correctly understand susceptibility, uncertainty, and the non-forecast disclaimer.
Measure task completion, errors, time on task, usability, and selected ISO 25010 characteristics.
Verify that no output independently declares a road safe, issues an evacuation order, or presents stale information as current.
Review preparedness content and evacuation wording with authorized experts before public-facing testing.

13. Deferred AI Assistant
The AI assistant is not part of the core SRS baseline. It may be considered only after the Expert System produces stable, explainable, expert-validated outputs. If later approved, the assistant will function only as a constrained explanation layer over approved knowledge; it will not classify susceptibility, modify Expert System results, issue evacuation orders, or answer real-time road-safety questions.
13.1 Minimum Prerequisites for Future AI Work
Approved and versioned Expert System rules.
Approved preparedness corpus and source register.
Defined grounding method and privacy controls.
Refusal and escalation rules for unsupported or real-time questions.
Evaluation set covering accuracy, grounding, safety, and hallucination.
Separate adviser approval for scope, cost, and implementation.

Step | Process | Proposed behavior
1 | Launch and disclaimer | The user opens the proposed Android app and reviews its purpose, limitations, data date, and non-forecast disclaimer.
2 | Select rainfall scenario | The user selects an approved rainfall intensity category and duration. The interface presents plain-language and visual guidance.
3 | Select location | The user selects an area from a map/list or positions a pin. PostGIS resolves the coordinates to the most specific approved geographic zone.
4 | Build facts | The backend combines the scenario, resolved zone, approved static zone profile, and active rule version into a working set of facts.
5 | Evaluate rules | The inference engine uses forward chaining to identify all active approved rules whose conditions match the facts.
6 | Resolve conflicts | Rules are ranked by geographic specificity, condition specificity, approval status, priority, and version. Unresolved disagreement produces an uncertain result.
7 | Return result | The system returns Low, Moderate, High, Very High, Uncertain, or Insufficient Data with the matched rule and limitations.
8 | Display explanation | The user sees why the result was produced, which source/rule was used, the applicable area, and when the data were last updated.
9 | Show preparedness guidance | A separate approved action table returns pre-event guidance. It does not independently order evacuation.
10 | Evacuation resources | If current verified records exist, the app lists nearby centers and may open external navigation.
11 | Degraded operation | If the API or network is unavailable, the app uses only the latest approved local cache and visibly displays its date and limitations.
