2. Solid Narrative Summary
The open-ended responses from Bacoor City residents paint a consistent and urgent portrait of
a community that has normalized living with flood risk — yet remains critically under-informed at
every stage of the flood cycle. The dominant emotional register across both Filipino and English
responses is one of resigned familiarity combined with barely suppressed anxiety.
The most pervasive and deeply felt gap is the absence of hyperlocal, real-time information.
Residents do not need to be told that "heavy rainfall is expected" — they can feel it. What they
desperately lack is the translation of that meteorological reality into ground-truth street-level
data: which roads are impassable, how quickly the water is rising, and which direction safety
lies.
A striking pattern emerges in the Tagalog-language responses: a culture of sudden onset.
Respondents describe scenarios where water rises around them before any warning reaches
them. The phrase "bigla na lang" (suddenly/without warning) appears repeatedly, suggesting
that rapid-onset flooding without advance notification is not an exceptional event but a routine
one. This reveals a structural latency problem in the existing warning chain.
The second major theme — evacuation center inaccessibility — reveals that even when
residents decide to evacuate, they face a navigational information vacuum. They do not know
where the nearest operational center is, whether it is accessible given current flood conditions,
or who to call for guidance.
Finally, the institutional trust deficit exposed in Theme 4 has significant implications for any
FloodSense deployment strategy. Residents who have repeatedly found official warnings
inconsistent with ground reality will be predisposed to distrust new information systems unless
they demonstrably outperform existing channels.

3. Critical Qualitative Findings
Gap 1 — The Latency Crisis: The word "real-time" appears in multiple independent responses,
confirming that delayed information is operationally useless. By the time announcements reach
residents, conditions have already changed. Any viable solution must deliver information in sub-
minute intervals during active events.
Gap 2 — The Specificity Vacuum: Existing systems communicate at the city or region level;
residents need block-by-block intelligence. The scale mismatch between available information
and decision-making needs is a fundamental design failure in current systems.
Gap 3 — The Evacuation Navigation Void: "Wala kaming mahanap na tamang hotline o
evacuation site." During the worst moments of a flood, residents are left without a lifeline to
reach safety. The absence of a single, authoritative, real-time evacuation directory is not a gap
— it is a crisis.
Gap 4 — The Credibility Collapse: When the gap between official communication and lived
experience is large, residents begin to discount all official warnings — including legitimate ones.
Rebuilding credibility requires a system that consistently validates ground truth.
Gap 5 — Fatalistic Adaptation: "Wala. Sanay na mga tao dito na bahain e." [Nothing. The
people here are used to flooding.] This single response encapsulates a dangerous cognitive
adaptation. Any FloodSense deployment must actively re-engage these fatalistic users by
demonstrating tangible, early protective value.

Pillar 3: Sociotechnical Implementation Challenges & UX/UI
Recommendations
Challenge 1: Varying Digital Literacy Across Barangays
The survey's geographic diversity (14 barangays, multiple socioeconomic contexts) implies
significant variation in smartphone literacy, data plan availability, and comfort with app-based
systems. Older residents and those in lower-income barangays may struggle with feature-dense
interfaces.
Recommendations:
• Design FloodSense with a "Crisis Mode" interface — a single-screen, large-text, high-contrast
view activated during active flood events showing only 3 things: current risk level, nearest
evacuation center, and one emergency action.
• Conduct co-design workshops with residents from Soldiers 4, Talaba 1, and Molino 3 before
finalizing UX flows, ensuring the interface reflects actual user mental models.
• Support SMS-based fallback functionality for users without smartphones or data access — critical
for reaching the most vulnerable residents.
Challenge 2: Connectivity Constraints During Peak Flood Events
Internet connectivity is frequently degraded or unavailable during heavy rainfall events in
Philippine urban areas. An app that requires constant internet connectivity will fail precisely
when it is most needed.
Recommendations:
• Implement offline caching of the most recent barangay risk map, evacuation center locations, and
protocol guides, refreshed every 15 minutes when connectivity is available.
• Use lightweight data protocols (compressed GeoJSON, not heavy map tiles) to minimize data
consumption and maintain functionality on 2G/3G connections.
• Enable SMS push alert integration via LGU partnership so critical risk level changes are delivered
even without app connectivity.
Challenge 3: Institutional Trust Deficit (Q18 Theme 4)
Residents who have experienced the PAGASA-vs-ground-reality credibility gap will approach
any officially-endorsed app with skepticism.
Recommendations:
• Integrate the community crowdsourced reporting feature from day one so residents see that the
app incorporates their real-world observations — not just government data.
• Display a model confidence indicator alongside risk levels to demonstrate data provenance and
transparency.
• Launch with a 30-day pre-flood-season calibration period (barangay awareness campaigns, test
alerts, community reporter recruitment) before the first operational rainy season.

Challenge 5: Barangay-Level Data Gaps for ML Model Training
FloodSense's ML flood susceptibility model requires historical flood data, elevation data (DEM),
drainage network data, and rainfall records at the barangay level. Data quality across all 14+
barangays may be inconsistent.
Recommendations:
• Partner with PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, and the Bacoor City BDRRMO for official data access under
a formal research MOU, establishing the app as an academic-government co-development
project.
• Use Sentinel-1 SAR imagery (free, cloud-penetrating) for historical flood inundation mapping
across barangays where ground truth data is absent.
• Implement a tiered confidence display system: barangays with sufficient training data display ML-
generated risk levels; data-sparse barangays display a "Limited Data" flag, setting honest user
expectations.
