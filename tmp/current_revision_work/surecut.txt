P0071 [Normal] ABSTRACT
P0072 [Normal] BERMAS, ZILDJIAN T., CORTES, MIKAELA ANNE V., GUINDAY, RAINA ISABEL M., MILLOSA, IVANA MARIEL B., SURECUT: A Mobile Application For Commuters And Drivers With Real-Time Vehicle Tracking And Route Visualization. Undergraduate Thesis. Bachelor of Science in Computer Science. Cavite State University - Bacoor City Campus, City of Bacoor, Cavite. June 2026. Adviser: Ms. Clarissa V. Rostrollo.
P0073 [Normal] The study “SURECUT: A Mobile Application for Commuters and Drivers with Real-Time Vehicle Tracking and Route Visualization” aimed to develop a sustainable mobile transport application and route visualization system designed to enhance the commuting experience and operational efficiency for the Pangkabuhayan Transport Service Multipurpose Cooperative (PTSMC) and the commuting public in Bacoor City. The system provides real-time vehicle tracking, live route visualization, seat availability updates, and NFC-based cashless payments. It addresses critical local transportation challenges such as prolonged passenger waiting times, heavy traffic congestion, difficulty in locating available vehicles, and the operational inefficiencies faced by drivers who traditionally rely on chance ("swertihan") to find passengers.
P0074 [Normal] The study employed the Agile Design Thinking methodology to facilitate iterative design, development, and refinement of the system through continuous user feedback, emphasizing data gathered during the initial Empathize phase. The research process involved identifying commuter and driver pain points through targeted surveys and interviews, analyzing localized transit user needs, designing the mobile platform, and evaluating its overall effectiveness. The system was engineered with distinct access levels and integrated modules for commuters and drivers, including live location tracking, an estimated arrival time (ETA) calculator, capacity indicators, and a secure payment gateway.
P0075 [Normal] The findings revealed that the lack of real-time data heavily impacts both commuter convenience and driver productivity, highlighting a need for centralized
P0076 [Normal] viii
P0079 [Normal] digital transit solutions. The developed SURECUT application successfully resolved these gaps by providing drivers with data-driven passenger indicators and offering commuters live, reliable transit updates. Evaluation results showed that 140 respondents rated the SURECUT system as “Excellent,” achieving an overall General Weighted Average (GWA) of 4.83 based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards.
P0080 [Normal] The results indicate that a synchronized, technology-driven transport platform can significantly optimize localized public utility vehicle operations. By aligning real-time tracking with cashless transactions and capacity monitoring features, SURECUT offers a highly scalable alternative to traditional, unmanaged transit systems. Based on these results, it is recommended that PTSMC deploy the application. SURECUT effectively bridges the operational gap between drivers and commuters utilizing their vehicles, thereby fostering a more predictable, reliable, and convenient public transport ecosystem.

P0147 [Normal] INTRODUCTION
P0148 [Normal] Public transportation plays a crucial role in urban mobility, yet inefficiencies within the commute norm lead to significant daily struggles for both commuters and drivers. In the Philippines, commuters' daily challenges primarily involve long waits and severe traffic congestion caused by a shortage of vehicles, exacerbated by an ineffective and disjointed public transportation network (Musa, 2023). Bacoor City, Cavite, faces the issues acutely due to its rapid urbanization and high volume of daily travelers. Many commuters in the city experience uncertainty about travel schedules and difficulty identifying available routes, resulting in lost time and frustration.
P0149 [Normal] To address the challenges, the researchers aimed to support the local transport sector by focusing on the Pangkabuhayan Transport Service Multipurpose Cooperative (PTSMC). As a cooperative managing modernized jeepney operations in the area, the PTSMC is responsible for maintaining service reliability; however, the cooperative currently faces a "visibility gap" in its operations. Interview reports with local drivers reveal that the current 7-10 minute mandatory dispatch protocol often
P0151 [Normal] 2
P0152 [Normal] forces vehicles to depart from terminals with low occupancy. Once on the road, locating commuters outside of terminals is largely a matter of "swertihan" (luck), as drivers lack the digital tools to identify areas of high commuter demand. The operational guesswork leads to unnecessary fuel consumption and potential income loss for the cooperative and its members.
P0153 [Normal] To bridge the gap, the researchers developed Surecut, a real-time vehicle tracking and route visualization application designed for both the PTSMC and the commuting public. The application serves as a central hub for collecting and disseminating crucial transit data, enabling stakeholders to make informed decisions. According to McCarty (2022), GPS is a foundational technology designed to assist in navigation, allowing people to travel efficiently, especially in unfamiliar locations where knowledge of local transportation options or terminal locations may be limited. By integrating the technology, Surecut provides commuters with real-time updates on vehicle locations, arrival estimates, and seat availability, empowering them to take proactive measures during their daily travels.
P0154 [Normal] In addition, the application provides an advanced platform for coordination between the transport operators and the commuters. For the drivers, the system offers features to manually update seat occupancy through a color-coded system (Red/Yellow/Green), preventing overloading and ensuring a safer boarding process. For the PTSMC management, the application provides a dedicated interface to monitor fleet activity and generate digital income reports. The centralized platform for tracking the status of vehicle units helps improve the overall effectiveness of the local transportation network.
P0155 [Normal] Generally, the study aimed to develop a sustainable mobile application that synchronizes the operational needs of the PTSMC with the convenience of the commuters in Bacoor City. The system functions as a digital management infrastructure that provides the cooperative with real-time fleet oversight and automated reporting features. By bridging the information gap through GPS
P0157 [Normal] 3
P0158 [Normal] technology and manual capacity indicators, Surecut reduces commuter anxiety and optimizes driver income per trip. Ultimately, the application transforms a disjointed transit routine into a data-driven service that supports the modernization of local public transportation.
P0159 [Normal] Statement of the Problem
P0160 [Normal] The study aimed to develop a real-time vehicle tracking and route visualization application for Modern Jeepney commuters and drivers in Bacoor, Cavite, along with a web-based management platform for the Modern Jeepney Operators of PTSMC (Pangkabuhayan Transport Service and Multipurpose Cooperative). The research sought to answer key questions using surveys and resident interviews.
P0161 [Normal] Public transportation in Bacoor, Cavite is numericized by significant inefficiencies that negatively affect both commuters and Modern Jeepney drivers. Commuters experience long and unpredictable waiting times, frequent traffic congestion, and difficulty identifying correct routes and appropriate vehicles. Survey results indicate that 54% of commuters encounter long waiting times, 84% often experience traffic congestion, and more than half report confusion regarding route selection and vehicle availability. The absence of real-time information forces commuters to rely on guesswork, resulting in frustration, wasted time, and unreliable travel experiences. How can real-time vehicle tracking and route visualization be integrated into a mobile platform to mitigate commuter uncertainty, reduce waiting times, and enhance navigational confidence?
P0162 [Normal] The challenges extend to Modern Jeepney drivers, whose operations are constrained by a mandatory 7–10 minute dispatch protocol (See Appendix 9. Interview Report) that often requires them to depart terminals with far-from-full commuter capacity. Drivers rely on chance (“swertihan”) to pick up additional
P0164 [Normal] 4
P0165 [Normal] commuters along their routes, leading to inefficient trips, increased fuel consumption, and potential income loss. Furthermore, drivers lack access to real-time data that could help them identify areas of high commuter demand and optimize route performance. How can a system that visualizes real-time commuter demand along a fixed route empower drivers to mitigate the inefficiency of low-occupancy trips, thereby converting a luck-based process into a data-driven strategy and ensuring accurate data collection for fleet management?
P0166 [Normal] In addition, transport operators face operational limitations due to manual fleet management processes, resulting in limited visibility over driver activity, route performance, and real-time conditions. The transition to modernized jeepney units has also introduced challenges for drivers adapting to digital systems, further affecting coordination and service efficiency.
P0167 [Normal] Given the concerns, there is a need for a technology-driven solution that integrates real-time vehicle tracking, route visualization, and operational monitoring. Thus, the study seeks to answer the question: How can a mobile application effectively bridge the gap between commuters seeking reliable transportation and drivers aiming to optimize operations in real time, thereby improving public transportation efficiency in Bacoor, Cavite.

P0206 [Normal] Scope and Limitation of the Study 
 The study developed “Surecut”, a transportation navigation and management system with real-time vehicle tracking, route visualization, and cashless payment via NFC. The system aimed to provide the Pangkabuhayan Transport Service Multipurpose Cooperative (PTSMC) and the commuting public in Bacoor City with an efficient and data-driven fleet management and navigation platform. An Android application was designed for commuters and fixed-wage drivers, while a web-based platform was created for the General Manager to oversee fleet operations and financial reporting.
P0208 [Normal] 11
P0209 [Normal] The PTSMC General Manager manages system functionality, including fleet asset supervision, the suspension of drivers with violations, and the generation of consolidated reports for the Cooperative Bookkeeper. Drivers could manually update seat availability (Red/Yellow/Green) and record total passenger counts to validate fuel consumption, while commuters could view real-time vehicle locations and process cashless payments through the Android app.
P0210 [Normal] The study identified specific operational boundaries. The system focused exclusively on the 27 Modernized Jeepney units acquired through the DBP loan, excluding the 11 traditional consolidated jeepneys. Successful implementation depended on the accurate digital tracking of passenger volume to protect the Cooperative’s financial interests, since drivers are receiving fixed wage regardless of ticket sales.
P0211 [Normal] The study also highlighted the need for data integrity and security. Commuter access was restricted to read-only geospatial data and personal wallet management to mitigate unauthorized alteration of operational records. It ensured that only the General Manager could modify route statuses or financial logs.
P0212 [Normal] However, the system had some limitations. It did not support offline mode since it was a real-time application relying on live GPS updates. Minimum requirements for the mobile application included Android 10 or higher (API Level 21+), 4 GB RAM, 500 MB of free storage, and an active internet connection. Hardware requirements included a GPS module, NFC (Near-Field Communication) capability for payment processing, and mobile data connectivity. The system’s tracking features were limited to the specific Paliparan to SM Southmall route (via Molino Road and Daang Hari), and any vehicle deviation beyond these endpoints would not be visualized. Additionally, the system did not include a direct communication or messaging feature between operators and drivers within the platform. The study also recognized a possible margin of error in fare transaction monitoring, particularly because the system supported both cash and NFC payment
P0214 [Normal] 12
P0215 [Normal] methods. Inconsistencies may occur when commuters select cash payment within the application but fail to complete the actual payment, or discrepancies may arise between the transactions recorded in the system and the total fare amount remitted by drivers.

Real-Time Vehicle Tracking for Passenger Awareness 
 Almeida et al. (2023) explored how real-time tracking of public vehicles (specifically city buses) can be leveraged to characterize traffic congestion and enhance passenger information systems. In Aveiro, Portugal, there has been a collection of four weeks' worth of bus tracking data, which was used by the authors as a basis for how average speed, travel time, delays, and number of stops can serve as indicators of traffic conditions. By using clustering techniques like DBSCAN and k-means, three levels of congestion have been identified. The authors mapped how congestion evolves over different times of the day and across segments of an urban network 
 Based on the results, it shows that real-time tracking data not only helps detect zones and times of consistent congestion, but it also gives feedback for commuters and planners. Therefore, in practice, systems similar to this reduce uncertainty of arrival times and waiting durations, directly improving commuter satisfaction and trust in the public transit system. The study emphasized the criticalness of transparency and data availability. When people can see vehicle locations and expected times, their commute becomes more efficient and reliable.

P0244 [Normal] Near Field Communication (NFC) in Public Transportation Security 
 A 2024 report by NFC Tagify explored how Near Field Communication (NFC) is modernizing public transportation, turning it into a more secure and efficient experience. By replacing traditional paper tickets with contactless cards and mobile payments, NFC technology dramatically speeds up transaction times and reduces long queues during rush hour. This shift not only cuts down on manual ticket inspections and fare fraud, but it also shows how public transit is adapting to today's digital landscape.
P0245 [Normal] Beyond efficiency, NFC brings a much-needed layer of security to transit systems. The technology relies on encrypted communication, making it extremely difficult for bad actors to duplicate tickets or tamper with terminal data. Furthermore, real-time tracking of passenger flow allows transit authorities to monitor crowds and quickly address any unusual activity. For passengers, the integration of mobile apps makes the process completely transparent, allowing them to store tickets and review their ride history with ease. While challenges like high installation costs and cybersecurity threats remain, NFC is proving to be a game-changer in building safer, smarter, and more user-friendly transportation networks.

P0252 [Normal] Real-Time Vehicle Tracking for Fleet Optimization

By implementing real-time tracking and monitoring systems, drivers can update seat availability in real time using simple indicators like red, yellow, and green signals. Additionally, management can use web dashboards to monitor fleet activity, track fuel usage, and validate passenger

P0257 [Normal] Integration of GPS in Urban Mobility 
 Paulsen (2021) examined how GPS technology has been integrated within commuter applications to support location tracking, route planning, and estimated arrival information. His analysis revealed that GPS-based platforms allow transport operators and application developers to reliably monitor vehicle positions, assess route performance, and provide travelers with dynamic, real-time updates on their journeys. Through the precise locational data afforded by GPS, users gain better awareness of their position relative to vehicles or stops, while systems can optimize route assignments or adjust operations in response to traffic conditions.
P0258 [Normal] The study underscored that GPS forms a foundational technological layer for more advanced mobility applications. Without accurate location and timing data, features like live arrival prediction, route deviation alerts, or adaptive scheduling would have limited effectiveness. Paulsen emphasized that the success of many contemporary commuter applications hinges on the robust integration of GPS, which enables transparency in operations and builds user trust in the services. However, he also noted challenges such as signal loss in dense urban canyons, GPS drift in tunnels or underpasses, and the need to pair GPS data with other sensor or contextual data (e.g., accelerometers, map-matching) to improve accuracy. Overall, Paulsen’s work helps bridge between the raw technology of GPS and the user-oriented mobility services that rely on it, showing how location intelligence underpins modern urban transport applications.

P0261 [Normal] Related Local Literature 
The Centrality of Automobiles in Transport Planning 
 Gatarin (2023) conducted a critical examination of Metro Manila’s transport crisis, emphasizing the entrenched dominance of car-centric planning in government infrastructure priorities. Despite the fact that only a minority of Filipinos own and drive private vehicles, most transport investments and policies disproportionately cater to automobiles. The misalignment has produced systemic inefficiencies, fragmented mobility options, and worsening congestion that affects the majority of commuters who depend on buses, trains, jeepneys, and other forms of shared and active transport. By privileging cars, state actors inadvertently marginalize low- and middle-income commuters, reinforcing inequities in access to safe, reliable, and affordable mobility.
P0262 [Normal] Importantly, Gatarin’s analysis highlighted the role of cultural discourses and civil society initiatives in shaping alternative pathways toward sustainable urban mobility. Efforts such as road-sharing projects and grassroots advocacy illustrate how Metro Manila residents are reimagining transport beyond car dominance. The study concludes that a people-centered, process-oriented, and ecologically balanced framework—one that prioritizes moving people rather than cars—offers a more inclusive solution to urban mobility challenges. By reframing responsibility as a shared societal task rather than a purely top-down government mandate, Gatarin’s work underscores both the failures of traditional car-centric planning and the possibilities for more equitable and liveable transport systems in cities of the Global South.

P0412 [Normal] Figure 2. Design Thinking Methodology (Daniel Wörle, 2025)
P0413 [Normal] The development of the Surecut application was guided by the Agile Design Thinking methodology, which shows a cyclical, human-centered approach to innovation. Unlike traditional linear methods, the methodology begins with empathy and definition, requiring deep engagement with commuters and drivers to understand their true needs and articulate the precise problems of public transport inefficiency. Development then proceeds through rapid, iterative cycles of ideation, prototyping and testing. The goal of each cycle is to build a high-fidelity prototype that quickly validates core features such as real-time tracking or optimized route suggestions against genuine user experiences. Subsequent iterations constantly refine the product, adjusting features and priorities based on immediate, qualitative feedback from end-users. The greatest benefit of Agile Design Thinking methodology is its strong focus on user validation, which ensures that the Surecut application remains
P0415 [Normal] 44
P0416 [Normal] inherently relevant and adaptable, significantly reducing the risk of building complex solutions that fail to meet the dynamic demands of the urban mobility sector.
P0417 [Normal] Empathize 
​ With the Empathize phase, the primary objective is to gain a deep, evidence-based, and empathetic understanding of the target users by setting aside initial assumptions (See Appendix 13. User Persona).
P0418 [Normal] To understand the core operational challenges and commuter experiences, the researchers conducted key informant interviews with the PTSMC Chairman, General Manager and Drivers, (See Appendix 8, 9, 10. Interview Reports) as well as surveys among commuters (See Appendix 6. Survey Results) who regularly ride the Modern Jeepney cooperative route (See Appendix 14. User Journey Map). The sessions revealed critical insights regarding the "Fixed Wage" model and the need for visibility between driver and commuter, and digital passenger counting to validate fuel costs. The output creates the empirical, human-driven basis of all requirements so that the product is sought after and directly solves pain points for users.
P0419 [Normal] Define 
​After the dedication is set in the Empathize phase, the researchers
P0420 [Normal] synthesize the gathered data to define specific pain points, such as the General Manager’s difficulty in fleet management, the commuters’ uncertainty of jeep availability, seat capacity, and the drivers' coordination issues during vehicle breakdowns. The problems were mapped to specific opportunities for technological intervention.

P0423 [Normal] Ideate 
 The Ideate phase of the Design Thinking process entails the process of generating a wide variety of potential solutions. Following on from the empathetic insights developed in the previous phase, the stage explicitly encourages "going wide" to explore many possibilities without early judgment.
P0424 [Normal] The result of this stage was a list of key solution features aimed at improving both the driver and commuter experience. Some key concepts are using a color-coded seat availability for commuters utilizing the application to be able to choose whether to board the approaching Modern Jeepney or board the next. Additionally, in order to provide real-time capability, the system provides widgets as buttons for the driver to use so that they can send required data updates (seat capacity) promptly. The concepts also include external sources by providing real-time route traffic information. Prototype is to convert the high-impact concepts of the Ideate step into low-fidelity, testable prototypes (See Appendix 11. Low Fidelity Wireframe).

​ The Test phase of Design Thinking is an iterative stage in which the
P0432 [Normal] prototypes are presented to real users. It is an active opportunity to receive direct feedback, observe user interactions, and understand what does and doesn't work in the provided solution. Its primary goal is to use the feedback to refine the prototype, gain further empathy, and challenge assumptions. This continuous loop of testing and refining is essential for identifying usability issues and ensuring the final solution genuinely meets the users' needs.

The purpose of the iterative cycles is to progressively enhance the solution's quality, usability, and fit with user needs.

P0468 [Normal] System Concept and Requirements 
​ The Surecut: Vehicle Tracking and Management System was developed to
P0469 [Normal] modernize public transportation operations, enhance fleet monitoring, and facilitate cashless transactions for the Pangkabuhayan Transport Service and Multi-Purpose Cooperative (PTSMC). The system is designed for both commuters and drivers, utilizing React Native and TypeScript for the Android mobile application, and React.js with a Node.js/Express backend for the web-based Operator Dashboard. The development process utilized the Agile Design Thinking methodology, emphasizing client-first development, collaboration, and flexibility, particularly suitable for complex projects.
P0470 [Normal] ​ The Surecut mobile application has specific system requirements. Based on the application's configuration, the minimum requirements include Android 7.0 (API Level 24) or higher, 3 GB RAM, 500 MB of free storage, and an active internet connection for real-time tracking. Recommended requirements are Android 11 or higher (API Level 30+), 4 GB RAM, 1 GB of free storage, and a reliable internet connection (Wi-Fi or 4G/5G). Hardware necessities heavily rely on an NFC (Near Field Communication) module for tap-to-pay wallet functionalities, a GPS module for location tracking, and mobile data connectivity.
P0472 [Normal] 52
P0473 [Normal] ​ The mobile app requires various system permissions to function optimally: INTERNET  for real-time  database  synchronization, ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION for continuous driver presence tracking even
P0474 [Normal] ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION for precise Google Maps route tracking, ETA calculations, and NFC to facilitate digital wallet payments.
P0475 [Normal] ​ For the web system (General Manager Dashboard), client-side requirements include modern web browsers like Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox, or Safari supporting modern JavaScript frameworks. The backend API requires a Node.js runtime environment. The database setup involves configuring a Firebase project with Firestore for persistent data (users, ride history, vehicles), Realtime Database for live driver online/offline presence tracking, and Firebase Storage for safekeeping driver credentials and LTFRB fare matrix documents.
P0476 [Normal] ​ The minimum server requirements for hosting the backend API (via platforms like Vercel or dedicated servers) include a Linux/Windows operating system, a dual-core CPU, 2 GB RAM, and 10 GB of disk space. Recommended server requirements are a quad-core CPU or better, 4 GB RAM, and 20 GB of disk space to handle concurrent real-time polling from the mobile apps. Firestore and Realtime Database setups require enabling the Firebase Blaze (pay-as-you-go) plan to support automated server functions and Google Maps API integrations.
P0477 [Normal] ​ Operating primarily along the transit corridors of Molino Road and Daang Hari connecting to Southmall and Paliparan, PTSMC faces operational challenges related to manual fleet monitoring, traditional fare collection, and inefficient End-of-Day (EOD) financial auditing. The rapid modernization of public utility vehicles (PUVs) demands a digital solution to streamline cooperative processes, prompting the development of the Surecut system to assist both the cooperative management and the daily commuters.

 The system development was executed using a modern full-stack architecture. The web-based Operator Dashboard was built using React 18 and Tailwind CSS for the frontend, while the backend was implemented with  and Express. The payment will then be recorded under the Driver’s name but is directly stored in the Operator’s Dashboard.
