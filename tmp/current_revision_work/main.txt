P0065 [Normal] INTRODUCTION
P0067 [Normal] Flooding is one of the most frequent and destructive natural hazards in the Philippines. The country's location within the typhoon belt exposes communities to rainfall events that cause inundation, displacement, damage to property, interruption of livelihood, and loss of life. National disaster statistics continue to document the widespread effects of natural hazards on Filipino families and communities (Philippine Statistics Authority [PSA], 2026). Bacoor City, Cavite, is among the localities that regularly experience flooding. Its low-lying areas and connection to the Imus and Bacoor river systems contribute to persistent inundation during periods of heavy rainfall. Flood-mitigation facilities in the Imus River Basin were established specifically to reduce peak floodwater volume in Cavite, including low portions of Bacoor and its tributaries (Japan International Cooperation Agency [JICA], 2021). These conditions establish the importance of accessible, understandable, and location-specific information that residents can consult before a flood event develops.
P0068 [Normal] To assess the information problem, the researchers surveyed 50 residents across 12 barangays in Bacoor City, with respondents drawn largely from historically flood-affected communities. The findings revealed that flood warnings are commonly
P0069 [Normal] disseminated through Short Message Service (SMS), social media, and barangay public-address systems, yet these channels do not consistently provide location-specific susceptibility information before flooding occurs. In the first preliminary survey, 10 of 50 respondents felt well-informed about flood-prone areas; the other 40 selected Somewhat Aware, Not Sure, or Not Aware at All. The report's mean ratings for satisfaction, timeliness, and clarity were 2.62, 2.70, and 2.72 on a five-point scale, within its Neutral/Sometimes interpretation band. Thirty-two of 50 respondents reported making a wrong decision because of insufficient flood information. A key informant interview with the Bacoor Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (BDRRMO) described historical flood records and Geographic Information System (GIS) materials maintained institutionally. A supplementary interview with the City Planning and Development Coordinator Office (CPDCO) identified relevant elevation, hazard, land-use, and barangay-boundary maps, while also identifying limits on access to machine-readable spatial files.
P0070 [Normal] A separate preliminary survey analysis report dated May 2026 summarized 40 respondents. It recorded 31 of 40 reporting flooding almost every rainy season, 9 of 40 considering themselves well-informed about flood-prone areas, and 26 of 40 selecting a dissatisfied category for available flood information. The report does not establish its exact collection dates, respondent-selection procedure, or overlap with the first survey; the two groups are therefore not pooled and their results are not treated as citywide estimates or final system-evaluation findings.
P0071 [Normal] FloodSense addresses this information gap through an Android application intended to connect expert-validated knowledge with the pre-event information needs of Bacoor City residents. A deterministic Rule-Based Expert System uses structured geographic and rainfall facts and conditional rules to produce an explainable susceptibility class for a user-confirmed scenario, subject to the provenance and validation of its underlying data. Rule-based expert systems apply rules from a knowledge base to known facts to reach a conclusion (Buchanan & Shortliffe, 1984). The current prototype demonstrates Low, Moderate, High, and Very High outputs using provisional or demonstration inputs; it does not yet establish approved operational classifications for Bacoor City. A separate Decision Support System (DSS) presents published preparedness guidance using the assessment as context without changing its class. Decision support systems organize relevant information for human judgment (Power, 2002); a full resident-facing situational questionnaire has not been verified as complete.
P0072 [Normal] The current FloodSense prototype presents scenario-based susceptibility assessment through an interactive map. The user selects or confirms a hypothetical rainfall intensity and duration, selects a supported location manually or through an optional one-time foreground location request, and explicitly requests an assessment. The map can display demonstration susceptibility zones and a neutral reference layer representing the 47 current barangay identities; the reference layer is technically validated for geometry but is not City-issued, is not City-verified, and contains no flood-susceptibility facts. The custom Web Administration System currently provides authenticated dashboard access, read-only review of map and rainfall-reference data, parameter-governance views, and controlled workflows for Decision Support System content, evacuation-center records, source records, and audit history. Ordinary administrators do not edit raw Expert System rules, priorities, conflict-resolution logic, or algorithm code. A fixed-schema CSV export-and-import workflow for permitted values is a confirmed requirement, but it is not yet implemented. FloodSense does not provide real-time monitoring, official forecasting, autonomous alerts, or automatic evacuation orders.
P0073 [Normal] Statement of the Problem
P0074 [Normal] This study aimed to develop an Android application that provides location-specific flood susceptibility assessment and pre-event preparedness support for Bacoor City through a Rule-Based Expert System, a Decision Support System, and a dynamic geographic map. The research questions were grounded in the survey of 50 residents across 12 barangays, key informant interviews with the BDRRMO and CPDCO, and a review of related literature and official information. These sources were used to establish community needs, institutional knowledge sources, geographic requirements, rainfall parameters, preparedness content, and the functional boundaries of the proposed application and its Web Administration System.
P0075 [Normal] In Bacoor City, flooding is a persistent and consequential reality. Survey results reveal that 92% of respondents have personally experienced flooding, with 58% describing the flooding as severe or waist-deep and higher. Despite this exposure, only 20% consider themselves well-informed about flood-prone areas. Mean satisfaction scores for information quality, timeliness, and clarity ranged from 2.62 to 2.72 on a 5-point scale, clustering at the lower end of the Neutral range and tending toward dissatisfaction. These information deficiencies have direct consequences: 64% of respondents reported making wrong decisions due to insufficient flood information, including taking flooded routes, failing to evacuate on time, and misjudging conditions. Open-ended responses further identified late or delayed information at 26% and insufficient or absent location-specific warnings at 20% as the leading concerns. How can an Android application using an Expert System provide residents with understandable, location-specific flood susceptibility assessments based on expert-validated knowledge, rainfall scenarios, and geographic information?
P0076 [Normal] The problem also concerns the accessibility and operational maintenance of institutional knowledge. The BDRRMO maintains historical flood records, exposure information, preparedness protocols, and evacuation-center information, while the CPDCO maintains geographic and planning data. Current dissemination practices, including social media, email, and barangay coordination, do not fully address the public's need for on-demand, situation-specific guidance. A community-facing platform must therefore communicate approved information while ensuring that rules, map objects, preparedness content, rainfall parameters, data sources, and administrative accounts can be maintained by authorized personnel. How can a Rule-Based Expert System, dynamic GIS-style map, Decision Support System, and Web Administration System organize this institutional knowledge into an explainable assessment and appropriate pre-event preparedness guidance for Bacoor City residents?
P0077 [Normal] Taken together, these gaps reveal a disconnect between institutional flood knowledge and the decisions residents must make before flooding occurs. The overarching question of the study is: How can FloodSense integrate expert-provided and expert-validated knowledge, rule-based inference, scenario-based rainfall input, interactive geographic visualization, Check My Area, evacuation-center information, and structured decision support to strengthen flood susceptibility awareness and pre-event preparedness in Bacoor City, while remaining a complementary public-information tool that does not perform real-time flood prediction or supersede official warnings?

P0086 [Normal] Theoretical Framework
P0087 [Normal] The theoretical framework of FloodSense connects a deterministic Rule-Based Expert System, geographic visualization, and a separate Decision Support System (DSS) to provide scenario-based flood-susceptibility assessment and pre-event preparedness guidance for Bacoor City. The Flutter application sends user-confirmed rainfall and location inputs to Django services backed by PostgreSQL/PostGIS. The framework identifies how controlled knowledge, inference, map presentation, and guidance relate; conceptual components in the architecture are not necessarily operational in the current prototype.
P0088 [Normal] Authentication and Onboarding Module. The architecture reserves a place for resident identity and introductory safety information. Resident registration, login, password recovery, and profile management are not complete user-facing functions; the current assessment flow does not require an account. Any future onboarding should distinguish hypothetical susceptibility information from live forecasts, official warnings, and evacuation orders.
P0089 [Normal] Rainfall and Location Input Module. The resident chooses Light, Moderate, Heavy, Intense, or Torrential rainfall and a duration of 1, 3, 6, 12, or 24 hours, confirms the hypothetical scenario, and explicitly requests an assessment. Location may be selected manually, adjusted with a map pin, or obtained once through foreground device permission; the proposed barangay requires confirmation or correction. The rainfall labels are interface categories, not verified live measurements, and operational thresholds require authoritative confirmation.
P0090 [Normal] Web Administration and Knowledge Management Module. The custom Admin portal supports authenticated dashboard access, read-only map-data and rainfall-reference review, parameter-governance views, and controlled DSS-content, evacuation-center, data-source, and audit workflows. Ordinary administrators cannot edit raw Expert System rules, priorities, conflict handling, or algorithm logic; technical Django Admin access is separate. A fixed-schema CSV exchange for permitted parameter values is a confirmed requirement, but its export, import, validation, interface, and tests are not yet implemented. Institutional approval and record provenance must be established before data are treated as authoritative.
P0091 [Normal] Django REST and GeoDjango Processing Module. Django and Django REST Framework connect the mobile and administrative interfaces to server-side validation, permissions, assessment, and data services. GeoDjango and PostGIS support polygon and point operations, including proposed barangay detection from a selected coordinate. The mobile application and custom Admin portal do not connect directly to the database.
P0092 [Normal] Rule-Based Expert System Assessment Module. A rule-based system stores domain facts and applies an inference procedure to reach a conclusion (Buchanan & Shortliffe, 1984). FloodSense deterministically matches the confirmed rainfall scenario and supported area against applicable rules, resolves matches and conflicts, and returns a susceptibility class with an explanation. The prototype demonstrates Low, Moderate, High, and Very High outcomes, but its provisional inputs and reference geography do not constitute an approved operational Bacoor City susceptibility dataset.
P0093 [Normal] Dynamic GIS Map and Check My Area Module. The resident map supports panning, zooming, manual selection and pin adjustment, scenario-dependent demonstration coloring, and a neutral 47-barangay administrative reference layer. The layer represents barangay identities through a documented derivation; geometry checks do not make it City-issued or a flood-hazard layer. The optional foreground location request is one-time, not background tracking. Evacuation-center markers and verified operational susceptibility maps must not be inferred from the current reference display.
P0094 [Normal] Explainable Result Module. The result reports the selected scenario, resolved area, susceptibility class, and the basis supplied by deterministic rule matching, subject to available source and validation information. The same inputs and active rules should yield the same class. An assessment is not a live flood prediction or official warning, and unsupported or unvalidated data must be identified rather than presented as confirmed local risk.
P0095 [Normal] Decision Support System Module. Decision support organizes information and guidance for human judgment (Power, 2002). FloodSense keeps published pre-event preparedness guidance separate from the susceptibility result: guidance may use the result as context but cannot change its class or issue an evacuation order. The current implementation exposes published guidance; a complete resident-facing multi-question decision tree remains unverified.
P0096 [Normal] Evacuation Center Resource Module. The backend and custom Admin portal maintain evacuation-center records. Resident-facing nearby-center discovery, distance ranking, live capacity, operating status, and safe-road routing have not been verified as implemented. Any future public display will require authorized, current records and directions to confirm emergency information with the responsible authorities.
P0097 [Normal] User Account and Preferences Module. Resident account and preference functions remain conceptual rather than completed mobile functions. The current location flow uses a coordinate for the active request and does not continuously track the resident or establish a resident assessment history. Future account features, if implemented, must remain separate from administrative authority over system knowledge and data.
P0098 [Normal] PostgreSQL and PostGIS Database Module. PostgreSQL/PostGIS stores relational and geographic records used by the backend, including reference boundaries, rainfall references, rule and DSS records, and administrative data. Database models alone do not establish that every corresponding resident function is available. Source status and validation are necessary before provisional or derived records can support public operational claims.
P0099 [Normal] Together, these components explain how confirmed scenario and area inputs pass through controlled services to deterministic susceptibility inference, map display, and separate preparedness guidance. FloodSense is intended to support pre-event understanding without replacing PAGASA, BDRRMO, barangay officials, or emergency responders.

P0155 [Normal] Related Foreign Literature
P0156 [Normal] Flood Susceptibility and Scenario-Based Assessment
P0157 [Normal] Flood susceptibility expresses the relative tendency of a location to experience flooding when relevant conditions are present. It differs from flood hazard, which concerns the probability and physical characteristics of an event; exposure, which concerns people or assets in affected places; vulnerability, which concerns the capacity to be harmed; and risk, which combines hazard and potential consequences. Membele, Naidu, and Mutanga (2022) found that flood-vulnerability mapping in developing countries varies substantially in terminology, indicators, spatial scale, data availability, and validation practice. Their review shows why a public system must name the type of assessment it performs and avoid presenting a general vulnerability or hazard map as a locally validated susceptibility conclusion.
P0158 [Normal] Scenario-based assessment supports planning by holding selected conditions explicit instead of implying that the system is observing an event as it occurs. A hypothetical rainfall intensity and duration can be treated as assessment inputs only when their meanings, permitted combinations, and geographic applicability are documented. This supports the FloodSense interaction in which the resident selects or confirms a scenario before requesting assessment. It does not establish that the scenario is current, predicted, or officially issued, and it does not justify importing weights or thresholds derived for another place.
P0159 [Normal] Deterministic Rule-Based Expert Systems and Knowledge Bases
P0160 [Normal] A Rule-Based Expert System represents domain knowledge through facts, conditions, and production rules and applies an inference procedure to derive a conclusion. Buchanan and Shortliffe (1984) provide the foundational distinction between the knowledge base and the inference mechanism. More recently, Papadopoulos et al. (2022) reviewed technologies and standards used in rule-based decision-support systems and described how encoded knowledge and rules engines evaluate case-specific facts. Although their review concerns clinical systems, the architectural distinction is relevant to FloodSense: flood knowledge, scenario facts, geographic facts, and explanations belong to controlled knowledge structures, while the inference procedure determines which eligible rule applies.
P0161 [Normal] Deterministic processing means that the same approved facts and the same published rule version should produce the same result. It does not imply that the rules are scientifically valid merely because the software executes them consistently. Knowledge acquisition, conflict handling, parameter definitions, version control, and expert or institutional validation remain necessary. Accordingly, ordinary FloodSense administrators may manage approved records and permitted values but do not rewrite raw rule conditions, priorities, conflict-resolution logic, or algorithm code. The confirmed fixed-schema CSV exchange is a future controlled-value workflow and is not evidence that such exchange is currently operational.
P0163 [Normal] Explainable Inference
P0164 [Normal] Explainability is necessary when a system produces a classification that may influence preparedness decisions. Kostopoulos, Davrazos, and Kotsiantis (2024) reviewed explainable decision-support approaches and emphasized transparency, interpretability, and human-understandable justification. Their review primarily addresses artificial-intelligence-based systems, but its communication principles apply to deterministic inference: a user should be able to understand the relevant inputs, the basis of the result, and the limitations of the evidence without being required to inspect source code or raw rule definitions.
P0165 [Normal] For FloodSense, an explanation should identify the confirmed rainfall scenario, resolved geographic context, susceptibility class, applicable basis, source or version information, and any limitation state. This supports accountability while preserving the distinction between explanation and rule administration. It also prevents a color or label from appearing authoritative without disclosing what it represents. Explainability does not remove the need for validated data, and a clear explanation cannot turn provisional information into an official local assessment.
P0167 [Normal] Decision Support and Preparedness Guidance
P0168 [Normal] A Decision Support System organizes information, alternatives, or recommended actions to assist human judgment. In flood preparedness, support may include understandable mitigation choices, household preparation, communication planning, protection of documents and valuables, assistance for vulnerable household members, attention to official advisories, and evacuation readiness. Mostafiz et al. (2022) observed that many web-based flood tools provide technical information but do not consistently provide locally actionable information for individuals and communities. Their review supports combining understandable risk communication with practical guidance while clearly stating the tool's authority and limitations.
P0169 [Normal] FloodSense separates the susceptibility assessment from preparedness guidance. The deterministic Expert System produces the assessment, while the DSS presents source-backed pre-event guidance without changing the susceptibility class. Information about evacuation facilities may support readiness when its source, verification date, location, and operational limitations are known, but distance alone does not prove route safety, current opening, accessibility, or available capacity. The DSS therefore assists preparation and does not issue an official warning, evacuation order, or guarantee of safety.
P0170 [Normal] GIS, Interactive Maps, and Spatial Databases
P0171 [Normal] Geographic Information Systems organize, analyze, and present information associated with locations. Their value in flood research depends on the suitability of the layers, coordinate reference systems, spatial resolution, classification methods, and validation procedures. An administrative boundary can resolve the name of a barangay or provide map context, but it contains no flood-susceptibility fact unless appropriate hazard or susceptibility evidence has been associated with it. This distinction is important when users may interpret any colored polygon as an official hazard layer.
P0172 [Normal] Spatial databases support the controlled storage and querying of geographic records. The PostGIS Development Group (2026) explains that PostGIS extends PostgreSQL with geometry and geography types, spatial indexes, spatial relationships, coordinate transformation, validity operations, and spatial processing. These capabilities are relevant to point-in-polygon queries, administrative-area lookup, map-feature storage, and provenance-linked geographic records. They provide technical infrastructure, not scientific validation: a valid geometry and a successful spatial query do not establish that a flood conclusion is institutionally approved.
P0174 [Normal] Flood-Risk Communication
P0175 [Normal] Flood-risk information must be understandable, actionable, and explicit about uncertainty. Mostafiz et al. (2022) identified gaps in web-based flood communication, particularly where tools provide information without sufficiently connecting it to decisions that residents can make. Clear terms, legends, labels, explanation text, and source notices help prevent non-specialists from treating model outputs as certain predictions. Communication should also distinguish long-term or scenario-based information from time-sensitive official warnings.
P0176 [Normal] These principles support a guided FloodSense flow rather than an unexplained technical map. Susceptibility colors must be paired with text, the selected scenario, and limitations. Preparedness information must direct users toward official channels during actual events. The interface must not imply that a neutral boundary layer, a demonstration color, or a provisional record represents a current flood condition.
P0178 [Normal] Data Provenance and Geographic Validation
P0179 [Normal] Provenance records where information originated, how it was transformed, which version was used, and what review or approval applies. Tullis and Kar (2021) connected provenance to ethical replicability and reproducibility in GIScience, including critical applications in which geographic data affect public decisions. Fakhruddin et al. (2022) similarly emphasized risk-informed data practices and the importance of understanding how disaster and climate data are produced, shared, interpreted, and governed.
P0180 [Normal] For FloodSense, provenance should accompany susceptibility parameters, geographic layers, preparedness items, and resource information. Technical geometry validation may confirm coordinate-system consistency, valid shapes, or feature counts, but institutional validation concerns authority, currency, meaning, and permitted public use. The two forms of validation are related but not interchangeable. This distinction prevents a technically processed dataset from being represented as official Bacoor City flood information without documented approval.
P0182 [Normal] Foreground Location and Privacy
P0183 [Normal] Mobile location can reveal a person's position and may contribute to identification or tracking. Android Developers (2026) distinguishes foreground access, one-time access, and background access and recommends requesting only the permission needed for the active function. This supports a FloodSense flow in which location is obtained only after a user initiates location selection or assessment, while manual selection remains available when the resident declines permission or prefers another area.
P0184 [Normal] Foreground acquisition does not imply continuous monitoring. FloodSense does not require background GPS tracking, automatic polling, or autonomous location-based alerts. Location handling should remain purpose-limited to the active assessment, use only the precision necessary for the supported geographic operation, and avoid retaining unnecessary movement information. The presentation must also allow the user to confirm or correct the detected barangay because device accuracy and boundary proximity can affect geographic resolution.
P0186 [Normal] Software Testing and Quality Evaluation
P0187 [Normal] Software testing establishes whether specified behavior is implemented under defined conditions, while quality evaluation examines broader product characteristics. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 defines a product-quality model with nine characteristics for specifying and evaluating information and communication technology products (International Organization for Standardization [ISO], 2023). The model can guide the selection of criteria for FloodSense, but it does not provide a ready-made acceptance result or replace an approved evaluation instrument.
P0188 [Normal] A defensible evaluation must identify the tested build, participants or evaluators, tasks, criteria, scale, procedure, valid responses, and interpretation method. Repository tests and interface demonstrations can support implementation verification, but they are not resident-acceptance findings. Because the final FloodSense survey and evaluation are not complete, Chapter 2 uses ISO/IEC 25010 only as literature supporting future assessment and does not report quality scores.

P0270 [Normal] The reviewed literature separates susceptibility from hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and risk. Membele et al. P0274 [Normal] Existing platforms already provide scenario simulation, official hazard-layer lookup, real-time monitoring, forecasts, and institutional planning. P0328 [Normal] Problem Identification and Solution Objectives
P0329 [Normal] Problem identification was completed provisionally using the first resident survey of 50 respondents conducted May 5–12, 2026, a separate 40-respondent report dated May 2026, and recorded BDRRMO and CPDCO interviews. In the first survey, 46 of 50 respondents reported flooding at least occasionally, 10 of 50 considered themselves well-informed about flood-prone areas, and 32 of 50 reported a wrong decision associated with insufficient flood information. In the second report, 31 of 40 said their area flooded almost every rainy season, 9 of 40 considered themselves well-informed, and 26 of 40 selected one of the two dissatisfied categories for existing flood information. P0333 [Normal] The Expert System evaluates supported geographic and rainfall facts through deterministic rules; its susceptibility output is not changed by DSS content. The DSS uses that result as context for published preparedness guidance. Administrative records, geographic processing, validation, provenance, and permissions support these flows, but ordinary administrators cannot directly alter raw rule conditions, priorities, conflict handling, or code. Fixed-schema CSV export and import for permitted values remain a confirmed but unimplemented requirement. Technical Django Admin access for developer or research maintenance is not the ordinary custom Admin portal.
P0334 [Normal] Demonstration
P0335 [Normal] The prototype supports a controlled demonstration with fictional areas: the user confirms a hypothetical rainfall intensity and duration, selects or confirms a location, explicitly requests an assessment, and reviews the explanation, map display, and available guidance. These functions demonstrate software behavior only; they do not validate real Bacoor susceptibility classifications or constitute final stakeholder evaluation. A demonstration using approved local data remains contingent on authorization and validation.
P0336 [Normal] Evaluation and Iteration
P0337 [Normal] Evaluation is partly prepared and partly planned. Backend and mobile automated test cases address core inference, validation, API responses, map behavior, location handling, and administrative access. Their presence does not constitute a completed resident usability study or final thesis evaluation. Further testing will verify rule matching and conflicts, scenario and geographic validation, API and mobile integration, map behavior, permissions, privacy, and separation of assessment from DSS guidance. Defects or validated stakeholder findings will feed a new design-and-development increment followed by retesting.
P0338 [Normal] ISO/IEC 25010:2023 will guide the planned product-quality evaluation, using characteristics relevant to the approved FloodSense instrument. It is a software product-quality model, not the research design or SDLC. Task-based review of the resident and Admin workflows may supplement technical checks. Participant selection, finalized instruments, responses, scores, and conclusions will be documented only after the new survey and evaluation are completed; the preliminary needs surveys cannot substitute for them.
P0339 [Normal] Communication
P0340 [Normal] Communication is ongoing through the manuscript and development records. The final study will report the implemented artifact, validated sources, completed tests, evaluation findings, and unresolved limitations. Claims about system quality, public utility, or Bacoor-wide applicability will depend on the corresponding evidence and local authorization.
P0341 [Normal] Participants of the Study
P0342 [Normal] The first preliminary survey report describes 50 Bacoor City resident respondents across 12 barangays, gathered May 5–12, 2026 through purposive-convenience selection. Its accompanying appendix contains the questionnaire and response summaries. A second, separate survey analysis report dated May 2026 covers 40 respondents identified with Bacoor barangay labels, but does not establish exact collection dates, how respondents were selected, whether any person also answered the first survey, or the source of every compiled response. Accordingly, the groups are described separately and their counts and percentages are not pooled.
P0343 [Normal] Key informants included a BDRRMO staff member interviewed on May 12 and May 24, 2026, and the CPDCO Officer-in-Charge interviewed on May 18, 2026. Their statements informed institutional workflow, data-availability, provenance, and public-communication requirements; they were not a completed test of the FloodSense application. The participants and results of the new resident survey and final system evaluation are not yet available and are not reported here.
P0344 [Normal] Data Gathering and Analysis
P0345 [Normal] The preliminary questionnaires asked about flood experience, information access, interpretation, and desired preparedness support. The interview transcripts were examined for reported office practices, data formats, access restrictions, and concerns about susceptibility interpretation. Development evidence was checked against integrated code and available tests to separate operating features from models, plans, diagrams, and proposals. Survey reports provide descriptive frequencies and percentages only for their own respondents; no combined sample, causal relationship, or citywide estimate is inferred.
P0346 [Normal] Institutional documents and geographic layers are recorded by source and approval status. The 47-barangay reference boundary may support selection and display after technical geometry checks, but those checks do not establish City verification or flood susceptibility. Provisional materials, unavailable operational datasets, and any unapproved inferred rules will not be represented as authoritative Bacoor City information.
P0347 [Normal] System Evaluation
P0348 [Normal] The planned evaluation will assess whether the prototype performs its documented tasks correctly and whether intended users can understand and use its scenario-based information. Technical verification and user-centered assessment will be reported separately: a passing module test does not establish usability, and a favorable perception rating does not establish geographic or institutional validity. The ISO/IEC 25010:2023 model will be applied only through a defined instrument and analysis procedure, with the actual participant group, completed responses, scoring, and results reported after collection.
P0349 [Normal] Research Limitations
P0350 [Normal] The preliminary surveys are limited by their small, nonrandom or undocumented selection procedures and uncertain overlap. The second report contains design recommendations that are not implemented FloodSense capabilities. The prototype also lacks an approved operational susceptibility dataset, completed resident accounts, resident-facing nearby-center discovery, fixed-schema CSV exchange, completed formal system evaluation, and production deployment. These limitations bound the interpretation of demonstrations and future findings.
